On the setting of a page

A band at the edge where the content goes out of focus before it runs off, rather than a fade to the colour of the paper. The difference is that a fade tells you the text has stopped and a haze tells you it has not.

The obvious way to build it does not work. One element, a backdrop filter and a gradient mask give a band of fully blurred content whose opacity ramps away: masking a blur fades how much of it you can see, and does nothing at all to how blurred it is.

So the ramp is built out of several blurs instead. Each layer blurs twice as hard as the one below it and is masked to its own band, and because a backdrop filter sees everything painted beneath it the blurs compound as they stack.

The doubling is what makes six layers enough. A linear ramp over the same range needs dozens of them before the steps stop showing, and every one of those is another backdrop filter for the compositor.

The bands overlap by one step on each side. Butted up against each other they leave faint but perfectly visible seams, which on a pale page read as printing bands.

What is left is a decision about taste: how far in from the edge it reaches, and whether there is any light in it. Over a photograph a little wash reads as distance; over paper it usually wants none.
