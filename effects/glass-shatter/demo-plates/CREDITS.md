The same photograph HalftoneMagnifier uses, and for a related reason. A crack
is only visible as a crack where it crosses something: the glint needs bright
ground to sit against, the channel split needs saturated colour to come apart
into, and the bend at a crack needs a long straight line running through it to
be bent. A ridge against a warm sky gives all three, and the horizon running
across the frame is the line that shows the refraction doing its work.

A dark or low-contrast photograph hides the lot. The cracks are still there and
you cannot see them, which looks like a broken effect rather than a broken
plate.
