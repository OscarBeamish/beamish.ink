Two pulls, the second once the first is dry

The register is the whole job. A sheet that has been through twice has to land within a hair of where it was, which is why the marks outside the trim exist.

The press

A Heidelberg platen from 1961, a proofing press for the long sheets, and about forty cases of foundry type. None of it is quick. That is mostly the point: a job that takes a day to set is a job somebody has read twice before it is printed.

The marks behind this are the ones that sit outside the trim, so each plate can be lined up against the last.
