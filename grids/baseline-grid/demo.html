Every line on the same rule

Vertical rhythm is the agreement that every line of type on the page sits on one of a fixed set of invisible rules. Two columns side by side then read as one spread rather than as two lists that happen to be adjacent.

A picture cropped to the rhythm

Eight baselines, whatever the photograph's own proportions are. The crop is the cost, and it is the cheaper of the two prices: the other is every line below the picture sitting between the rules instead of on them.

Multiples, not judgement

Line heights, margins, the space above a heading and below it: all of them whole numbers of one unit. The moment a value is chosen by eye the rhythm is gone, and it is gone for everything after it.

A heading takes two baselines above and one below, because a heading belongs to what follows it rather than to what it interrupts.

The first line, not the box

A heading with a tall line-height has space above the letters that pushes the text off the rule while the box itself measures perfectly. It is the hardest of these to see without the overlay on, which is why there is an overlay.
